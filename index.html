Know Your Enemy

The Most Common Digital Threats Today

Understanding what types of threats exist is the first step to protecting yourself. Here are the ones your antivirus software absolutely needs to defend against.

🦠

Malware & Viruses

Malicious software designed to damage, disable, or gain unauthorized access to your device. Can spread through downloads, email attachments, or infected websites without you even knowing.

High Risk
🔐

Ransomware

Encrypts your files and demands payment to restore access. Ransomware attacks have increased by 400% since 2020 and now target individuals and businesses equally without discrimination.

Critical
🎣

Phishing Attacks

Fraudulent emails, texts, and websites designed to steal your login credentials, financial information, and personal data by impersonating legitimate companies and services you trust.

High Risk
🕵️

Spyware & Stalkerware

Software that secretly monitors your activity, keystrokes, and conversations without consent. Often installed alongside free software downloads from unofficial or unverified sources.

Medium Risk
💥

Zero-Day Exploits

Attacks targeting previously unknown software vulnerabilities before the developer has released a fix. These are among the most dangerous threats because traditional signature-based detection often misses them.

Critical
🌐

Browser Hijacking

Malicious extensions or software that takes control of your browser, redirects searches, injects unwanted ads, and harvests your browsing data — often installed without any visible warning.

Medium Risk
🤖

Trojans & Backdoors

Programs that appear legitimate but secretly create hidden access points allowing hackers to remotely control your device, steal files, or deploy additional malware at will.

High Risk
📡

Man-in-the-Middle

Attackers who intercept communication between your device and websites — especially on public WiFi — to steal passwords, financial data, and private conversations in transit.

High Risk

How to Set Up Complete
Device Protection

Follow these steps to ensure your devices are fully protected from the moment you install your antivirus software to long-term safe browsing habits.

1
Choose the Right Plan for Your Needs Consider how many devices you need to cover, whether you need a VPN, and whether dark web monitoring or parental controls matter to you. Use our comparison table above to choose confidently.
2
Remove Any Existing Antivirus First Running two antivirus programs simultaneously causes serious conflicts and dramatically slows your device. Fully uninstall any existing security software before installing your new one.
3
Run a Full System Scan Immediately After installation, run a complete scan before doing anything else. This catches any existing threats that may have been lurking on your device before protection was in place.

5
Schedule Automatic Weekly Scans Set your antivirus to perform a full system scan automatically once a week at a time when you are not using your device. Regular scanning catches threats that real-time protection occasionally misses.
Security Best Practices

Habits That Keep You Genuinely Safe

Antivirus software is essential, but your own habits determine 60% of your digital security. These practices separate people who get hacked from people who never do.

01

Use Unique Passwords for Everything

Reusing passwords is the single most exploited security vulnerability among regular users. Use a password manager to generate and store a unique, strong password for every account you have without needing to remember any of them.

02

Enable Two-Factor Authentication

Even if a hacker obtains your password through a data breach or phishing attack, two-factor authentication prevents them from accessing your account without also having your phone or physical security key.

03

Keep Software Updated

The vast majority of successful malware attacks exploit known vulnerabilities in outdated software. Enable automatic updates for your operating system, browser, and all applications to close these gaps before attackers can use them.

04

Think Before You Click

Phishing attacks account for over 80% of reported security incidents. Before clicking any link in an email or text message, verify the sender's actual email address, hover over links to see the real destination, and when in doubt, go directly to the website by typing the address manually.

05

Use a VPN on Public WiFi

Public WiFi networks at coffee shops, airports, and hotels are frequently monitored by attackers looking to intercept credentials and financial data. Always use a VPN when connecting to any network you do not control to encrypt your traffic completely.

06

Back Up Your Data Regularly

The most devastating consequence of a ransomware attack is losing irreplaceable data. Maintain at least two backups of important files — one in cloud storage and one on a disconnected external drive that you update regularly.

Do I really need paid antivirus if Windows already has Defender?
Windows Defender has improved significantly and provides decent basic protection. However, independent lab tests consistently show it scoring 5-15% lower on threat detection than premium options. Paid antivirus also adds features Defender lacks entirely — VPN protection, dark web monitoring, banking browsers, identity protection, and cross-platform coverage for Mac and mobile devices. For users who do any online banking, shopping, or handle sensitive data professionally, paid antivirus is worth the investment.
Can I run two antivirus programs at the same time for better protection?
No — running two antivirus programs simultaneously is actively harmful and will not improve your protection. The two programs will conflict with each other over system resources, flag each other's processes as suspicious, and significantly slow your device while providing worse protection than either program would alone. Always fully uninstall your old antivirus before installing a new one using the manufacturer's official removal tool.
Which antivirus has the smallest impact on system performance?
ESET Internet Security consistently ranks first for minimal system impact in AV-Comparatives performance tests. Bitdefender and Kaspersky also score extremely well in this category. Norton and McAfee have historically had heavier footprints, though both have improved considerably in recent years. If you have an older or lower-specification device, ESET or Bitdefender are the safest choices for maintaining good system speed.
Is antivirus software effective against ransomware?
Modern premium antivirus software provides significant but not absolute protection against ransomware. Products like Bitdefender and Norton have dedicated multi-layer ransomware shields that protect commonly targeted folders and detect encryption behavior in real time. However, no antivirus catches 100% of ransomware — especially new variants. The most important ransomware protection remains maintaining regular offline backups of your important files regardless of which antivirus you use.
Do I need antivirus on my Mac or phone?
Mac malware has increased by over 400% in the past five years and macOS is no longer the safe haven it once was. A multi-platform antivirus like Bitdefender or Norton provides worthwhile protection. For smartphones, the risk is primarily from malicious apps, phishing links, and unsecured WiFi rather than traditional viruses. A mobile security app with anti-phishing and VPN capabilities provides meaningful protection even though classic malware is less of a concern on iOS and Android.
What should I do if my device is already infected?
Disconnect from the internet immediately to prevent further data transmission. Download Malwarebytes Free from a clean device, transfer it via USB, and run a full scan in Safe Mode. For ransomware specifically, do not pay the ransom — check nomoreransom.org for free decryption tools first. If the infection persists after scanning, a full operating system reinstall from a known-clean backup is the safest path to a guaranteed clean system.
